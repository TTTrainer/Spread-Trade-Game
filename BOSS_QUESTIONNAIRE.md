# Boss design questionnaire

Every third round is a **Review**: a boss round. Today there are 10 of them (below). You want them to feel like Balatro's boss blinds or Hades' bosses: a few memorable bosses that cycle, each with its own personality, threat and payoff. Your answers decide how they get built.

**How to answer:** type under each question, as short as you like. "No idea" or "you decide" is a fine answer. Skip anything that doesn't spark anything. Examples are only there to show the range; you don't have to pick one.

## 1. The big picture

1. When you think "boss round" in a trading game, what feeling do you want? (Examples: dread before earnings night, a puzzle to solve, a duel against a rival, surviving a crash.)
2. Should a boss be **a market situation** (what the market is doing), **a person** (a character with a style), or **both** (a character who brings a market)?
3. Balatro shows the next boss in advance so you can prepare your build. Should you see the next Review **a whole quarter ahead**, **at the shop just before it**, or **only when it starts**?
4. Should you be able to **reroll or skip** a boss (at a cost)? If so, what should it cost?
5. How many different bosses before they start repeating in one year? (Today: 3 per year from a pool of 9, plus the Annual Review.)

## 2. Personalities

6. Should bosses be **characters** with names, portraits and lines (like Director Kessler and COMPLY-3000)? If yes, who should they be? A few seeds to react to:
   - a short seller who bets against whatever you hold,
   - a market maker who widens spreads when you trade,
   - a CNBC-style pundit whose calls move the market (in the game layer only),
   - a risk officer who audits every position,
   - a rival trader (Bradley?) who trades the same cards and you have to beat,
   - the Fed chair, the "whale", an algorithm, a meme-stock crowd.
7. Which of the current 10 do you like, and which feel flat?
8. Any real market events you want as bosses? (Examples: an earnings season, an FOMC week, a VIX spike like March 2020, a short squeeze, a flash crash, a quad-witching expiration.)

## 3. What a boss does to you

Remember the rule that bosses can't change the market itself: prices, volatility, fills and events stay real. Bosses change the **game layer** (score, rules, information, tools, size limits).

9. Which kinds of pressure sound fun? Mark ✔, ✘ or ? next to each:
   - [ ] limits on **which trades** you can place (e.g. only bearish trades, only one structure type, no credit spreads)
   - [ ] limits on **size or risk** (e.g. half the risk cap, a tighter Max-Loss Line)
   - [ ] **hidden information** (e.g. no chart studies, no IV rank, blind dates, hidden P/L until close)
   - [ ] **time pressure** (e.g. fewer trading days, must open all trades on day 1, a clock that won't pause)
   - [ ] **score rules** (e.g. losses count double, only winners closed at plan score, mult capped)
   - [ ] **disabled powerups** (e.g. your leftmost cartridge is switched off, one family stops working)
   - [ ] **a second goal** besides the points target (e.g. beat SPY, finish green, never cross 50 stress, no stop declined)
   - [ ] **a duel** (beat a rival's P/L on the same cards)
   - [ ] **forced decisions** (e.g. every position must be held through an event)
   - [ ] other: ___
10. Should a boss's rule be **one simple twist** (Balatro style: "all clubs are debuffed") or **a set of 2–3 effects**?
11. How punishing should failing a boss be? (Today: a failed Review ends the run.) Alternatives: lose a cartridge, lose cash, carry extra stress, a harder next quarter.

## 4. Rewards

12. What should beating a boss give you? Mark the ones you like:

## 5. The Annual Review (the final boss)

## 6. Presentation

# Jacob's answers (part 1, questions 1–13)

Recorded as sent; questions 14–22 are still to come.

1. Boss rounds should feel **scary** while they're on. Today nothing feels different; in Balatro the whole board changes. A different color scheme would help, and so would **blocking off one piece of information per boss** instead of an effect that's hard to understand.
2. **People**, creative and on theme. A character may bring a market *and* a debuff, but it must be apparent during play (on the chart is best, since that's where you work, without clutter). The characters are creative riffs on the **12 Pillars of Institutional Financial Planning**:
   - Core engine: 1 Know Your Burn · 2 Emergency Liquidity · 3 Risk Management · 4 Real Estate Capping (home ≤ 35% of net assets)
   - Allocation & tax: 5 Financial Independence Target · 6 Tax-Balanced Balance Sheet · 7 Education / CapEx Savings · 8 The Portfolio Matrix
   - Legacy & protection: 9 Asset Protection · 10 Estate Planning · 11 Debt Management · 12 Annual Rebalancing
3. A **Balatro-style menu before each month**: the expected target, plus your build and strategy so you can adjust. The final month of each quarter is the boss.
4. **Reroll the boss**: $10 and one use, rising with each new boss to $25, then $40, then $60.
5. **4 quarters a year, one boss at the end of each**, and a way to continue into further years with rising difficulty (like Balatro's endless mode).
6. Use the **full spectrum** of the 12 pillars: each character can be blatantly for or against its principle, as long as it reads as a normal person to someone who doesn't get the reference.
7. The current 10 are fine but have **no real impact**. Work their themes into the bosses where it isn't forced.
8. Real market events are welcome **if they illustrate a pillar** (e.g. a real-estate slump for the real-estate pillar). Say so if it's too hard or a bad idea.
9. Pressures that sound fun:
   - ✔ limits on **size or risk** (half the risk cap, a tighter Max-Loss Line)
   - ✔ **hidden information** (no studies, no IV rank, blind dates, hidden P/L until close)
   - ✔ **time pressure** (fewer days, all trades on day 1, a clock that won't pause; if the clock won't pause, intraday trading must get easier)
   - ✔ **score rules** (losses count double, only plan-closed winners score, mult capped)
   - ✔ **disabled powerups** (leftmost cartridge off, one family stops working)
   - ✔ **a second goal** (beat SPY, finish green, stay under 50 stress, no stop declined), but only with a clear path to it; don't pile goals on a struggling player
   - ✔ **a duel** against a rival's P/L on the same cards, with a constant miniplayer of the rival's screen so the win feels earned by watching, not a pop-up at the end
   - (not picked: limits on which trades, forced decisions)
10. **One simple twist** per boss. No confusion.
11. A failed Review **still ends the run** (Jacob will say if that doesn't feel good).
12. Rewards: **extra shop cash**, **a choice of 2–3**, and **a permanent buff**.
13. Yes, a **style bonus**, but make its condition apparent.

# Agreed design (after the follow-up questions)

- **One twist per boss**, shown on the chart; the market type's old Review rule is dropped. Bosses never limit trade direction.
- **Neutral desks always get a flat chart**: every Condor- and Calendar-desk lineup includes at least one range-bound card (judged only from past prices).
- **No clock pressure**: the game trades on daily prices (no intraday), so no boss stops you pausing. The Margin Clerk's twist is half the usual risk per trade.
- **No historical-event bosses** (the data starts in 2019 and has no real-estate stocks).
- **Selection**: Q1-Q3 draw from pillars 1-11 (no repeats within a year); Q4 is always the Rebalancer. The boss is known from the quarter's first day.
- **Reroll**: once per boss; $10, $25, $40, then $60.
- **Markets** keep their existing logos; portraits come from an art list (placeholders until then).
- **Controller**: all running P/L hidden, including equity; profit targets and stops still fire and show their amount.
- **Executor**: days to expiry show as "?" until the trade is open; max profit and max loss stay visible.
- **Landlord**: the total mult is cut by 35%.
- **Allocator**: second goal of up to 3 different structure types (all of them on 2-structure desks).
- **Rebalancer** (final): beat SPY this round, with the SPY line on the chart; target x1.25. Missing SPY still counts as "You survived".
- **Difficulty**: about 9 in 10 for a typical boss and 7 in 10 for the final, for a good player.
- **Month menu** before every round: target, build (reorder/sell), next boss, reroll, plus run-wide settings such as stop and target levels. The tutorial skips it except for the boss case file.
- **Rewards**: shop cash, pick 1 of 3 free cartridges, and a boss-themed voucher; a style bonus on top, with its condition shown during the round.
- **Failed boss ends the run** (one switch to change it).
- **Duel** (Early Retiree) is built last and fully tested.
- **Developer test checklist**: a dev-mode list of things to try (each boss, the menu, rewards...), each with a button that sets it up, a tick and a note.
